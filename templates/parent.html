What the Numbers Mean

What do RELIABLE, PARTIAL and STILL SETTLING mean?

A stat built from only a handful of at-bats can look great or terrible just from luck. A kid who gets 2 hits in the first 4 at-bats of the season isn't necessarily "a .500 hitter" — that's only 4 tries, and it's just as likely to have been a lucky couple of swings as a sign of real skill.

The more at-bats a number is built from, the more it reflects how the player actually hits, rather than one hot or cold stretch. So every number below is tagged with how much to trust it yet:

Batting average only counts hits. This counts every way of reaching base safely — a hit, a walk, or getting hit by a pitch — divided by all the player's trips to the plate.

What is slugging %?

This measures power, not just contact. Instead of every hit counting the same, a single is worth 1 "base," a double 2, a triple 3, and a home run 4 — those are added up and divided by at-bats.

A hitter who mostly singles will have a slugging % close to the batting average; a hitter who racks up doubles and home runs will have one noticeably higher.

Higher is better.
