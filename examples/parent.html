What the Numbers Mean

A stat built from only a handful of at-bats can look great or terrible just from luck. A kid who gets 2 hits in the first 4 at-bats of the season isn't necessarily "a .500 hitter" — that's only 4 tries, and it's just as likely to have been a lucky couple of swings as a sign of real skill. The more at-bats a number is built from, the more it reflects how the player actually hits, rather than one hot or cold stretch. So every number below is tagged with how much to trust it yet:

RELIABLE About as many chances as a full, well-attended youth season provides — enough to call this a real pattern, not a lucky or unlucky stretch. (This is a youth-season bar, not the much bigger sample pro-baseball research uses to call a stat fully “stabilized” — nobody at this level ever reaches that.)
PARTIAL Getting there, but there's still some randomness mixed in. Treat it as a meaningful signal, not the final word yet.
STILL SETTLING Too few chances so far for this number to mean much on its own — one or two more games could swing it a lot. Read it as “what happened,” not “who the player is.”
